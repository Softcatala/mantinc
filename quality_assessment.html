Dataset quality assessment · 4 August 2026

Catalan Drift

A structural audit, 50-item deterministic inference run, provisional manual review, scientific comparison with LCB, and evidence-based system and dataset score cards.

Release decision: needs revision 300 items5 categories Revision 04155e1

Executive result

Structurally useful, scientifically under-validated

The corpus builds cleanly, covers targeted Catalan drift conditions, and separates the nine models already reported in the README. Its current automatic scorer is not reliable enough for model ranking: it has high provisional precision but very low recall for isolated language leakage.

The automatic 90% pass rate is not a clean-Catalan rate. The ten RAG outputs passed automatically, while only 4/10 were provisionally language-clean. The manual labels are single-review estimates, not native-speaker gold labels.

01 · Methods and scope

Work completed

  • Audited all 300 rows, 1,119 forbidden terms, duplicates, persona/workflow balance, and 240 RAG chunks.
  • Ran make build twice; both outputs had SHA-256 e1177378…2bbd653.
  • Ran 50 deterministic inferences with gemma3:12b, temperature 0, seed 0, and a 512-token cap.
  • Reviewed every automatic failure and at least two automatic passes per category; all ten RAG responses were reviewed.
  • Applied a yes/no prompt-realism rubric to the same 50 prompts.
  • Computed provisional precision, recall, Wilson intervals, and Cohen's κ from 23 reviewed responses.

Not completed: native-Catalan double annotation, adjudication, and inter-reviewer agreement. Metrics derived from manual labels below must therefore be treated as diagnostic estimates.

Scientific conclusion: Catalan Drift is currently a focused exploratory benchmark and is less scientifically established than LCB. Its multi-turn and RAG coverage is more relevant to a specific Catalan deployment, but that relevance does not compensate for unvalidated scoring.

07 · Interpretation and release

Needs revision

Supported claims

  • The dataset is structurally valid and deterministically built.
  • The five categories cover distinct Catalan drift-inducing settings.
  • The tested model produced more automatic failures on advanced cross-lingual prompts.
  • The scorer misses isolated Spanish and English leakage.
  • The historical nine-model scores show overall spread.

Unsupported claims

  • That 90% of responses are clean Catalan.
  • That current scores reliably rank models.
  • That results generalise beyond Catalan or these five categories.
  • That a drift pass implies completion, correctness, or grounding.
  • That the provisional manual labels are a gold standard.

Highest-priority work

  1. Double-annotate at least 150 stratified responses with native-Catalan reviewers and adjudicate disagreements.
  2. Calibrate span-level leakage detection against that gold set.
  3. Resolve the 33 absent forbidden terms and review homographs/legitimate borrowings.
  4. Repair the nine under-specified sampled prompts and measure realism again.
  5. Separate language, completion, truncation, and RAG-grounding metrics.
  6. Evaluate at least three model families under a repeated stochastic condition.

Evidence artifacts

  • Structural audit
  • Row-level flags
  • Inference summary
  • Raw model responses
  • Manual response review
  • Score-card calculations
  • Reproducibility manifest
  • Controlled scorer checks
